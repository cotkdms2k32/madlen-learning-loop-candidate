# Madlen Growth Intern Case Study — Strategic Response

## 1. Competitive analysis: the two closest benchmarks

I selected **MagicSchool** and **Brisk Teaching** because both now cover teachers, students and school-level adoption—not just one isolated AI utility. They represent the two clearest competitive pressures on Madlen: a broad school AI platform and an embedded, low-friction workflow layer.

| Dimension | MagicSchool | Brisk Teaching | Implication for Madlen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core strength | Broad “AI operating system” position: 80+ teacher tools, 50+ student tools, school controls and major LMS/SSO integrations. | Works inside tools teachers already use; Brisk Extension, student-facing Boost and planning hub Next form a connected experience. | Do not compete on tool count. Make the teacher–student learning loop and curriculum fit easier to understand and prove. |
| Teacher–student connection | Teacher-led student activities and MagicStudent, with district moderation and integrations. | Teachers set objectives, guardrails and scaffolding; students enter Boost without separate accounts and teachers see progress. | Madlen must show teacher control and student progress as one continuous experience, not two product menus. |
| Curriculum position | Schools can upload pacing guides, standards and calendars so outputs reflect district context. This is configurable alignment; the public sources reviewed did not evidence a named, first-party IB template comparable to Madlen's PYP planner. | Supports attached standards and district-uploaded scope and sequence. The public sources reviewed did not evidence named first-party IB support; it is flexible, school-configured alignment. | Madlen's explicit AP/IB/Common Core/A-Level/IGCSE/SAT positioning and visible PYP unit planner are a credible differentiator—but only if demos show the actual aligned output and source. |
| Main weakness / opening | Breadth can make differentiation depend on governance, integrations and tool volume; Madlen can tell a more pedagogically coherent story. | Extension-first convenience is powerful, but curriculum setup can depend on uploaded school material and paid district workflows. | Lead with “one learning outcome across before/during/after,” supported by local curriculum knowledge and international-program options. |

**Counterargument:** MagicSchool and Brisk can both be configured around a school's curriculum, so “we support IB” is not a durable moat by itself. **Response:** Madlen should not claim exclusivity; it should prove lower setup cost and higher pedagogical fidelity with ready-made, bilingual, named-framework workflows and a teacher-visible link from outcome → activity → feedback.

## 2. Unique value proposition (maximum two sentences)

**Madlen brings the whole learning journey together—from preparing a lesson to understanding what each student needs next. It adapts that journey to the curriculum a school actually teaches, giving teachers back time and students more purposeful support.**

Why this wording: it explains the outcome before the technology, remains understandable to a school leader or parent, and does not rely on an unprovable “only platform” claim.

## 3. One strategic initiative: “One Outcome, One Learning Loop” pilot

### Objective

Turn Madlen's broad capability into one visible, measurable school result in a 30-day pilot. Each participating teacher chooses one learning outcome, prepares a lesson in Madlen, assigns one guided student activity, and reviews one aligned assessment/feedback cycle.

### Why this initiative

A generic free trial rewards tool sampling and makes Madlen look like another AI toolbox. A guided loop demonstrates the differentiation that competitors can otherwise blur: the same outcome stays connected before, during and after learning. It also produces evidence a school decision-maker can use.

### Pilot experience

1. A 20-minute launch session: choose outcome, grade, curriculum/framework and class.
2. Week 1: generate and edit the lesson/material.
3. Weeks 2–3: assign a teacher-controlled student activity and review where students get stuck.
4. Week 4: run an aligned assessment, review feedback and receive a one-page impact summary.

### Success metrics and decision rules

| Metric | Target | What it proves |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Teacher activation | ≥70% complete lesson + student activity in 7 days | Setup is understandable and the loop starts quickly. |
| Loop completion | ≥50% complete all three stages in 30 days | The integrated lifecycle is real, not marketing language. |
| Median teacher time saved | ≥90 minutes per completed loop, self-reported with baseline | Value is meaningful without repeating an unsupported universal claim. |
| Student activity completion | ≥65% of assigned students | The experience reaches learners, not only teacher preparation. |
| Teacher D30 intent to continue | ≥60% answer 8–10/10 | Early retention signal before a long sales cycle. |
| Pilot-to-next-step | ≥30% of schools request a paid proposal or expanded pilot | Growth relevance. |

**Guardrail:** also track the percentage of AI output teachers materially edit. High edit rates may mean healthy professional review, but repeated edits to facts, level or curriculum alignment signal a product-quality problem. Success is not “more generations”; it is a completed learning loop with less teacher effort and usable student evidence.

## 4. Product prototype

The companion web application, **Madlen Learning Loop – Candidate Prototype**, implements the three required flows in one responsive experience. Its scope is deliberately narrow: no accounts, storage, analytics or fake curriculum claims—only the three functional AI interactions a reviewer must be able to complete.

**Headline:** One lesson. One learning loop.

**Body:** Prepare with purpose. Guide without giving answers away. Turn student work into the next clear step.

**CTA:** See the learning journey with Madlen.

**Caption:** Great teaching is not three disconnected tasks. Madlen brings preparation, guided learning and feedback into one coherent journey—so every step starts from the same learning goal. #EdTech #AIinEducation #Teaching

The visual uses three connected stages—Before class, During learning, After learning—rather than a collage of product features. That makes the strategic idea understandable in a few seconds and reusable in a school-facing presentation.
